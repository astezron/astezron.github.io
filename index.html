About me

Where geostatistics meets machine learning

I am a PhD student in the Mining Engineering Department and the Advanced Mining Technology Center (AMTC) at Universidad de Chile. My research focuses on enhanced classification of regionalized data using geostatistics and machine learning, improving mineral resource estimation techniques for efficient and sustainable mining.

Before moving to Santiago, I studied and trained as a geologist in India. I completed my B.Sc. at Cotton University, Assam, and my M.Sc. at Banaras Hindu University, Uttar Pradesh. I also worked as a Technical Officer in Hydrogeology at the Public Health Engineering Department (PHED), Government of Assam, India.

Research

Classification of regionalized data

My work deals with the combination of machine learning and geostatistics for supervised and unsupervised classification problems in a spatial context. While traditional machine learning classifiers ignore spatial correlations, geostatistics can leverage these correlations to improve classification performance and quantify classification uncertainity. Specifically, our methodology relies on the deployment of a set of proxies at the data locations, calculated through geostatistical simulation with filtering of short-scale variability, which are then used as feature variables for machine learning classification.

I am a recipient of the EPEC International Doctoral Scholarship Award , offered by the School of Postgraduate and Continuing Education, Universidad de Chile, (2026–2029) and the ANID Fondecyt Project (2026–2029), a research project funded by the Chilean National Agency for Research and Development (ANID).

Related interests: spectral (co)simulation, machine learning for resource estimation.
