Teaching machine learning to see space

Below is a synthetic cross-section through a porphyry copper deposit. Drillholes sample it every 20 m, and each sample is logged as one of four alteration types. A machine-learning classifier has to predict the alteration of each sample from its assays. Change the settings and see how much geostatistical features help it.

What happens behind the scenes

Four steps from drillhole to alteration model

STEP 1

Drillhole data

Samples along each drillhole carry assay grades (here Cu, K and S) and a logged alteration type: potassic, phyllic, propylitic or unaltered.

STEP 2

Geostatistical proxies

Nearby samples are correlated. Using the spatial correlation, each sample gets a proxy value that keeps the regional signal and filters out short-scale noise. In the research this is done with geostatistical simulation and filtering; here a simpler spatial weighting stands in.

STEP 3

Machine learning

A tree-ensemble classifier learns the alteration type from the raw assays plus the proxies. The research uses XGBoost (gradient boosting); this page uses a small random forest so it can run in your browser.

STEP 4

Spatial validation

Models are tested on whole drillholes they never saw, not on random samples. Neighbouring samples look alike, so a random split would overstate how good a model is.

About this demo. Everything here is synthetic and simplified: the deposit is generated with spectral simulation of a Gaussian random field, and the numbers illustrate the idea rather than reproduce published results. For the real method and results, see Borah (2025), Minerals 15(11), 1128 ↗.
